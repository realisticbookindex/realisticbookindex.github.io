      How to work it

      Every control, what it does, and what it leaves alone. The section after this one explains why the tool is built the way it is; this one explains how to drive it.

      The shape of the page

      Controls run across the top. Below them the set sits on the left and the Index on the right. Changing any control updates the Index list and the pool line at once. Only Build a set rewrites the set, so you can move the controls around under a set you have already built without disturbing it.

      The order to work in

      Say how long the set is. Say what may be drawn from. Say what the set must and must not hold. Build. Read the audit. Move or swap by hand. The tool never edits a set you built by hand unless you press Build a set again.

      Set length

      A number from 3 to 16, starting at 8. It is a target rather than a promise. If the pool will not order a chain that long, nothing is built and a line appears naming the longest it will order, so the next press succeeds.

      Indexes in play

      Eight chips, one per index, pressed to toggle. 1A and 1B start on. This is the first gate: a title has to sit in a lit index to be drawn. Jam platforms are the one exception, below.

      What each index is

      The eight divisions are not eight grades of quality. Five different tests seat them, and two of the eight sit off the instrumental frequency line entirely. A title is seated once and appears in one index only.

      indexnameseatswhat seats a title there
      1ACore Crossover93weekly or near-weekly instrumental call, and it lives in the instrumental world
      1BCore-Adjacent53the same repertoire as 1A, called below weekly. The line between them is frequency, not kind and not difficulty
      2Should-Knows116present in a published source, or admitted on the practitioner filter below the Index 1 threshold and tagged [P]
      3Suggested Supplement141present in a source and supplying breadth the rest of the Index does not already hold. Recording frequency, critical standing and historical importance admit nothing here
      4Roots and Swing99pre-1945 New Orleans, Armstrong, stride, Basie and swing still called in traditional and mainstream settings. Seated by period rather than by call rate, which makes it a house rather than a lower tier
      5AVocal Core, Essential80clears the vocal call filter and not the instrumental one. A title that clears both is seated at 1A to 3 instead and carries the italic mark there, so this division is a residue by construction rather than a ranking. The three bands are set by how many of the sources carry the title, after the residue is fixed
      5BVocal Core, Expected78
      5CVocal Core, Extended69

      1B is not weaker material than 1A. Measured on the recording ranking, Index 1B's median rank is 159 against Index 1A's 185, so the second tier ranks better than the first. What separates them is how often the tune gets called, which is a different question from how well established it is.

      Index 4 and Index 5 are not the bottom of the list. Index 4 is a period house: one idiom, one feel, one terrain, played by people who came for exactly that. Index 5 is the vocal book, measured on a different question from the four above it. Lighting either changes what kind of night you are building rather than lowering the standard of it.

      This tool carries all 403 seats at 1A to 3 and all 99 at Index 4. It carries 92 of the 227 at Index 5 - the titles a call instrument reaches, plus the jam platforms - because the vocal divisions are uniform on form, feel and terrain by construction, and more of them would widen the repertoire without widening the contrast.

      Pool - jam platforms

      The thirty-six boldface titles, the ones marked as counting off with no singer. They sit at Index 5A, 5B and 5C - ten, seventeen and nine. The control has three settings, and the one to watch is the first.

      Leaving this on with their index with no Index 5 chip lit is why a platform sometimes appears and sometimes does not. Nothing is broken when that happens; the tap is closed.

      A platform is exempt from call likelihood in all three settings. That exemption is not a setting and cannot be switched off. Twenty-six of the thirty-six sit in the Untouched band, which the filter drops, so without the exemption the mark would be invisible whenever the filter was on. Call likelihood goes on binding everything that is not a platform: with all eight indexes lit and the filter on, no Untouched title reaches the pool except a platform.

      Pool - a singer can front it

      Skill path

      Four chips, all on at the start: fnd foundational, 62 titles; emg emerging intermediate, 58; int intermediate, 47; off the path, the 427 the path does not place. Turning one off removes those titles from the pool. Phase filters; it is not a seventh axis, and the builder does not try to alternate hard and easy.

      One at most, each

      Six kinds, each on a three-way select. up to 1 is the ceiling and the default. exactly 1 makes the builder keep drawing until one is present. none bars the kind from the set entirely. The counts are blues 70, minor blues 15, rhythm changes 31, bossa 16, groove 19, ballad 84, and 234 of the 594 titles carry at least one.

      Build a set, Clear, Export

      Build a set draws at random from the pool and keeps the first arrangement that breaks none of the rules above, trying up to six hundred times. Clear empties the set and leaves every control where it is. Export saves the set as a plain text file with each title's seat, band and idiom; if the browser refuses the download, the same text goes to the clipboard and the button says so.

      The set, and the line between two tunes

      Each tune shows its position, its title, its chips, and three buttons on the right - move up, move down, remove. Between every pair sits a line carrying the joint score and the axes the two tunes hold in common. A line reading repeat is under 25 and wants one of the two moved; thin is 25 to 49 and is playable once; anything unlabeled cleared 50.

      The line above the set reads the weakest joint, the average, how many capped tunes are in and how far apart the closest two sit, and the phase mix.

      The Index panel

      Everything in the pool, longest first by index order. The search box matches on title. The three selects narrow by idiom, form and feel. Clicking a title appends it to the end of the set, and a title already in the set shows a dot instead of a plus. The foot of the list reads how many are showing, how many are in play and how many are in the Index; past three hundred it shows the first three hundred only.

      The pool line

      Under the controls: how many titles are in play out of 594, how many are platforms, how many a singer can front, then the per-index split. An index showing 3 20/141 is admitting 20 of its 141 seats, and a bare number means the index is admitting all of them.

      When it will not build

      A red line appears above the controls rather than a half-broken set. It names the percentage of pairs inside the pool that clear a joint of 50, gives the longest set the pool will order, and says which lit indexes are bringing less than their full seat count. Setting the length to the number it names and pressing again always works. Widening the pool, lighting another index or switching a cap off are the other three moves.

      What is kept, and where

      The set you are holding is stored in this browser and comes back on the next visit. Nothing else is stored and nothing leaves the page. The control positions are not stored; they start where this page starts them every time.

      How a joint is scored

      Two tunes back to back are compared on six axes. Each axis pays its weight only when the two tunes differ on it. A joint scoring 100 changes everything; a joint scoring 0 is the same tune twice under two names.

      The axes and their weights

      axisvalueswt
      Formblues · minor blues · rhythm changes · other30
      Feelswing · Brazilian · Afro-Caribbean · even eighths · odd or triple · backbeat25
      Terrainfunctional · modal or vamp · blues-based20
      Idiomsongbook · bop · modern · groove · Latin15
      OriginSongbook · jazz composition · Latin American · imported · traditional6
      Tier1A · 1B · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5A · 5B · 5C4

      Form outweighs everything because two blues in a row is the oldest mistake on a bandstand and the one a room hears first. Tier is worth almost nothing between two adjacent tunes and matters only in the window rule below.

      What the marks mean

      Repeat - under 25. The two tunes agree on form, feel and terrain, or on enough of them that a listener hears one long tune. Move one.

      Thin - 25 to 49. Playable, and two thin joints in a row is not.

      Clear - 50 and over. Something the room can hear changed.

      A tune is also weighed against the one two back, which has to clear 25. Contrast every other tune is not contrast; it is a two-state oscillation, and a set that alternates blues and bossa scores perfectly at every joint while being one of the more tiring sets you could call.

      Window rules

      Four consecutive tunes may hold at most two of one named feel and may not be all four of one idiom family. Five consecutive may not be all five from one origin.

      Swing is exempt from the feel rule. It is the default and not a color - 489 of the 594 titles carry it. Three bossas in four is a problem; three swing tunes in four is a Tuesday.

      With the anchor rule on, every four must hold something from Index 1A or 1B. It goes inert when neither index is in play.

      One at most, each

      Six kinds are capped at one per set, and each can be switched off entirely or required. A set holding one of each is the teaching case, and it needs eleven slots to hold them apart.

      kindread fromtitles
      bluesthe printed BLUES block, and a compiler reading at Index 470
      minor bluesthe printed MINOR BLUES block, and one at Index 415
      rhythm changesthe printed RHYTHM CHANGES block, and one at Index 431
      bossaidiom Latin jazz - Brazilian16
      grooveidiom Soul jazz / boogaloo or Jazz-rock / fusion19
      ballada compiler reading, printed below84

      Modal is no longer capped. Twenty-eight titles carry a modal or vamp terrain across four idiom families, five feels and three form blocks, and two of them back to back rarely sound alike. The terrain axis already pays 20 at the joint, which is the right instrument for it.

      The bossa cap is the Brazilian titles, not the bossa frame. Sixteen titles carry the Brazilian idiom. Twelve more are jazz heads written over a bossa or samba frame - Blue Bossa, Song For My Father, Ceora, Recordame, Una Mas, Ana Maria, Spain - and those stay uncapped, because a hard-bop head on a Latin frame is not what a second Jobim tune does to a set.

      One title is in two caps. Nothing Personal at Index 3 is a minor blues and a fusion tune, so it fills both.

      Spread them out keeps any two capped tunes from sitting back to back and places required ones as far apart as the length allows. The builder says when they will not fit rather than returning a set that breaks its own rule.

      One from each index is the answer to a set that keeps leaving an index out. Ticking an index makes its titles eligible, not present: the builder draws at random from whatever is in play, so a small index gets picked in proportion to its size. Index 4 with call likelihood on contributes 15 titles against Index 1A's 91 and Index 1B's 53, and lands in about half of all eight-tune sets. With this on, the builder keeps drawing until every index that has titles in the pool is represented, and says so if there are more indexes than slots.

      The pool, and what the two toggles do

      Call likelihood, on, holds the pool to the three bands the instruments reach - Reconciled, Logged and Distilled. Off, it opens to Sub-Floor and Untouched as well. On all eight indexes that is 325 titles against 594. The reach is uneven by design: Index 1A loses two, Index 3 falls from 141 to 20 and Index 4 from 99 to 15, which is what a depth index and a period house respectively are.

      Jam platforms are the thirty-six boldface titles - the ones FM.2 marks as counting off with no singer. They are their own tap, on three settings:

      settingwhat reaches the pool
      with their indexthey arrive only when 5A, 5B or 5C is in play, alongside everything else those indexes bring
      always inall thirty-six, whatever the indexes, the bands and the skill path say
      only platformsthe pool is the thirty-six and nothing else

      The last two ignore every other control on purpose. A platform is a tune you can call with a band that has never played together, and gating it behind the index that happens to print it would be the wrong instrument. Twenty-six of the thirty-six are reached by no call instrument at all, so under with their index and call likelihood on they would otherwise never appear.

      This mark answers the same complaint the next section does, from the other side of it. A jam platform is a title from the vocal divisions that a band can count off with no singer present. The vocal option below is an instrumental a singer can step out in front of. One hands a room the singer's repertoire without a singer; the other hands a singer the room's. A jam session needs both, because who is standing there is not known until the tune is called.

      And the other direction: a singer can front it

      The complaint about jam sessions runs both ways. Instrumentalists say singers call corny tunes; singers say the band will not play anything they can sing. A great many instrumental titles carry a lyric - the vocalese writers set words to most of the bebop repertoire and later writers to the modal and hard bop repertoire - and those are where the two sides meet.

      The volume marks a narrower thing. Its italic records a title holding a current independent life as vocal repertoire, which is a stricter test and one that printed counts depend on. This control reads a wider file, data/vocal_option.tsv in the repository - instrumentals a singer can front where known recordings carry the vocal: Afro Blue, Work Song, I Remember Clifford, Waltz for Debby, Twisted, Goodbye Pork Pie Hat. A lyric that exists without a standard vocal version is left out, so the file is a short answer to a real question rather than a long one to a theoretical one.

      All four instrumental indexes are read - sixteen at 1A, ten at 1B, twelve at Index 2 and five at Index 3, forty-three in all. A title is entered only where a named recording carries the vocal, and a single vocalese treatment does not count as one. Index 3 returns fewest because vocalese is where its lyrics came from, and a one-record treatment is not a lyric a singer can call. Index 5 has none: it is the vocal index, and every seat in it is already a singer's tune.

      A platform in the pool is not a platform in the set. The builder draws at random from whatever is in play, so thirty-six titles among five hundred will land sometimes and not others. Require a jam platform makes it certain: the builder keeps drawing until one is in the set, and says so if none is available.

      Index 4 whole, Index 5 not yet

      Index 4 carries all 99 of its seats. The volume's style page classifies 25 of them and stops, so the other 74 are read in data/setbuilder_extension.tsv in the repository, a separate file marked as a compiler reading for this tool with no locus in the volume. Under the rule that file states - provenance is who wrote the tune, idiom is the tradition it is called in - the 74 divide 29 Early jazz, 30 Swing, 14 Straight-ahead and one Cool. They are all in the Untouched band, so with call likelihood on, 15 of the 99 reach the pool and with it off all 99 do.

      Index 4 orders cleanly on its own up to six tunes, and not seven. Measured, not asserted: 17 percent of its pairs clear a joint, so a chain of seven runs out of room. The 74 additions widened what it holds and did not widen what it contrasts, which is what a period house is - one idiom, one feel, one terrain, played by people who came for exactly that. Mixed with Index 1A it orders eight without difficulty.

      Index 5 still enters at its reached and platform sets only - 48, 26 and 18 against 80, 78 and 69 seated. The vocal divisions are uniform on form, feel and terrain by construction, which is measurable rather than a complaint: of every pair inside Index 5B, none clears a joint of 50, and the best available anywhere in it scores 46. More vocal titles would widen the repertoire and not the contrast, so they are not here yet.

      Two taxonomy values exist only for these two indexes. Early jazz / New Orleans is a fourteenth idiom and Traditional a fifth origin; neither is needed anywhere in Indexes 1A through 3, and neither appears in the table at App. M.12, which is scoped to those. Form is a compiler reading here rather than documentary. Indexes 4 and 5 print no BLUES or RHYTHM CHANGES blocks, so the form heads on these pages are named by the compiler, not read off a printed block.

      Idiom families

      songbook & pre-bop bop lineage modal & after groove Latin

      The thirteen idioms of the style classification collapse to five families here. Bebop, cool and hard bop are one family because a room does not hear the seam between them across two tunes; it hears the seam between a bop head and a bossa.

      The skill path

      Phase filters the pool; it is not a seventh axis. Difficulty is not variety, and a set that alternated hard and easy would be no better ordered for it. Turning phases off narrows what the builder may draw on, which is what a student working one layer wants and what a teacher setting a jam wants.

      What this does not carry

      Key and tempo. Both matter more than half the axes above and neither is settled for this repertoire - a vocalist moves every key and a leader sets every tempo on the night. The two rules that would use them are the leader's, not this tool's: do not run three tunes through the same tonic, and do not put two burners together.

      The ballad reading, in full

      The volume carries no ballad mark and makes no tempo claim, because a leader sets every tempo on the night and a vocalist moves every key. These eighty-four are a reading of which titles are habitually counted off slow. The reading is published. It is the ballad column of data/style_classification.tsv in the repository, and check 88 of code/verify.py holds it against the roster - 75 marks in the style file and 9 more in the Index 4 extension, every one on a seated row, no third value. It is still not a mark on the Index and no printed count depends on it; it is published so that a reader can disagree with it in the same way they can disagree with anything else in that file. A title absent from the list is not thereby fast; it is a title this reading did not claim.
