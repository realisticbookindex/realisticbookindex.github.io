The Realistic Book Index Evidence

Three measures, one curve

The eight tiers were fixed before any of these three instruments was opened. A 2010 survey of thirty-seven US educator-performers, a 2019 log of 2,038 calls at 160 London sessions, and fourteen degree programs' published repertoire lists, retrieved in August 2026. None cites the others. All three fall off at the same place.

What each instrument reaches, tier by tier

Share of each tier's seated titles that the instrument names. The top two tiers are the instrumental core; below them the agreement thins, which is the shape the tier definitions predict and the reason the core is stated as a narrow claim.

Two departures from the descent, stated here rather than left to be found. At Index 1B the survey reaches 94.3% where the log reaches 73.6%, which is the tier the two instruments most disagree about and the reason 1B is printed as core-adjacent rather than core. At Index 5 all three rise again: it is the vocal division, measured on a different question, and its reach figures are not a claim about instrumental call.

The one measure that does not join

Take two indexes, pair every title on the first with every title on the second, and ask how often the measure puts the second index's title ahead. Ties count as half. Near zero the measure agrees with the volume. At fifty it cannot see a difference between the two tiers. Above fifty it reverses them. The recording ranking built the pool and drew no tier line, so this is a held-out test rather than a circular one.

Table
